# B-roll, research, and asset selection

Select visuals that help viewers understand the current spoken idea. Narration remains the time anchor in continuity mode. A speaker's pause or an arbitrary number of elapsed seconds is not by itself a reason to insert a cut.

## Find the visual job

For each candidate cue, write a one-line purpose: demonstrate a process, locate a place, compare quantities, identify an object, show evidence, establish atmosphere, or relieve a visually static passage. Use the transcript and inspected footage to decide whether the best treatment is the presenter, a diagram, an image, a short B-roll clip, an annotated screen recording, or typography.

Search by concrete subject, action, setting and composition; include period and location where accuracy depends on them. Avoid literal keyword matches that contradict meaning. A generic hospital exterior does not establish that a named treatment is effective; a stock trading screen is not historical evidence of a specific market event. Label illustrative, generated, simulated or archival material when the audience could mistake its role.

Prefer the user's cleared assets and sources with clear reuse terms. For each external candidate inspect the actual asset page, rights holder, license, download quality and restrictions. Search results and thumbnails establish neither permission nor the quality of the downloaded file. Do not treat public visibility, lack of watermark, or a free download as a license.

## Keep an asset ledger

Record the local asset ID and file, source page and creator, retrieval date, license URL/version or permission evidence, required credit, permitted use, modification/redistribution restrictions and any model/property-release requirements relevant to the use. Record purchases only after authorization. Preserve receipts or license evidence privately with the editing project. Track fonts, music, icons and sound effects as well as footage.

For user-supplied files, record the user's rights representation; do not claim independent clearance. If terms are absent or incompatible, mark the asset unresolved and replace it with cleared material, an original diagram, or the presenter. Do not include unresolved assets in a deliverable described as cleared. An AI-generated image does not become documentary evidence, and generation may have its own provider and likeness restrictions.

## Evaluate and fit

- Check semantic fit, factual context, source credibility, resolution, compression, crop potential, motion, color, lighting and continuity with surrounding shots.
- Inspect faces, logos, text, hands and diagrams for unintended errors. Verify charts against the underlying data, including axes, dates and units.
- Test the target aspect ratio. Favor a useful composition over filling the frame by cutting off the relevant subject.
- Inspect the actual clip interval, including its first and last frames. Loop only if repetition is natural; never slow down synchronized speech to fill a visual gap.
- Default to muting unrelated B-roll sound while original narration continues. Any diegetic sound or music addition needs a deliberate mix.

Cache approved files locally so rendering does not depend on changing search results or expiring URLs. Use stable asset IDs in the timeline. Plan reveal, hold and exit around reviewed spoken spans, with enough hold for viewers to read or recognize the content. Avoid hard transitions during a sensitive explanation unless they improve comprehension.

Use free/local options: user-cleared media, compatible free assets or agent-created original graphics. Do not require stock credentials or substitute a paid generator. When research or assets are unavailable, simplify within the selected style and mark any preview placeholder. Before final delivery, replace every placeholder or identify the output as incomplete. Never hide a missing/uncleared asset or switch away from Cutout behind a successful render status.
