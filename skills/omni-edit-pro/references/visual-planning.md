# Visual planning

Read after source inspection and relevant speech or shot/action analysis, before acquiring assets or building compositions. Produce a small creative brief and an actionable visual plan in the editing workspace. Use [transcription-sync.md](transcription-sync.md) for timing mechanics and [broll-assets.md](broll-assets.md) for asset research. Use [Reference analysis](reference-analysis.md) for an explicitly designated sample or [Auto direction](auto-direction.md) when choosing from the source.

## Understand the existing edit

Watch representative passages with audio where present: the opening, a dense explanation or action sequence, an emotional turn, changes in scene format, and the ending. Inspect existing cuts, shot changes, demonstrations, captions, and graphics before proposing additions. Describe what viewers need to follow and what the footage already communicates. In silent footage use inspected interface/action/shot anchors; do not invent dialogue, sound or presenter requirements. Distinguish deliberate quiet from a technical defect; pauses and reactions may carry meaning.

For talking-head and narrated footage, retain the original footage as the continuous underlying track where applicable. Preserve all dialogue, order, timing and lip-sync unless the user authorizes an editorial change. Other genres retain original content, meaning, sequence and intentional timing by default. Record cut, reorder, speed, crop and source-image-replacement permissions separately; map authorized changes through one source-to-output timeline. A request to match a sample's energy is not permission to shorten speech, replace the source image or build a montage.

Use the selected [composition](editing-styles.md): B-roll belongs in Split's explanation region or a suitable limited graphic, not an unauthorized full-frame replacement for Overlay or a presenter. Adaptive permits scene-appropriate composition choices while preserving the same framing and editing-permission boundaries. Do not create jump cuts, remove pauses or shorten content to fit a graphic.

## Choose a coherent direction

Record creative mode and its evidence, intended audience, viewing context, output ratio, language, tone and desired takeaway. Separate **treatment** (type hierarchy, palette, density, motion, pacing and sound) from **composition** (Split, Cutout, Overlay or Adaptive). A reference's typography can be adapted to an explicitly selected Overlay without adopting the sample's cutout layout or cut frequency.

Resolve each decision from explicit user overrides, then explicitly selected preset defaults, then applicable reference attributes, then Auto/source evidence. Record the choice and provenance before variant/render jobs. A changed orientation does not change composition; a changed composition does not reset orientation. Keep All's six default pairs unless the user narrows or overrides them. For Adaptive, preserve the source display aspect when no stronger destination applies, including square/custom shapes. Do not add Adaptive to All automatically.

In Reference mode translate timecoded, confidence-labeled observations into content-specific rules. Respect reference-to-source and attribute mappings. Keep measured, inferred and unknown findings distinct; do not force a sample's exact event times or rapid cut rate onto protected source timing. Report deliberate deviations such as fitting an uncropped wide source into a vertical reference treatment. Missing required evidence remains unresolved rather than becoming a silent Auto choice.

In Auto mode select one treatment with a short source-grounded reason. Reuse its visual vocabulary across scenes within this video, adapting density and emphasis to the material. Route mixed formats by scene instead of forcing one presenter layout across the entire file. Inspect and direct every source in a batch independently: different topics need their own visual metaphors, explanation mechanisms, motion and transition choices, not the previous video's effects with a new title or color. A requested brand system can remain consistent, and variants of one source can share a treatment; neither removes the need for source-specific cues and review.

Choose each video's palette from the actual topic, source imagery/background, subject and clothing, intended tone, and applicable user, brand or reference instructions. Record the evidence for background, text, accent and semantic colors. Test luminance and color contrast on representative changing source backgrounds and moving subject/clothing, including bright/dark passages and cutout edges; revise backgrounds, accents or backing treatments when separation fails. Blue or navy is not an automatic default, and cycling palettes is not a substitute for direction. Blue remains valid when an explicit instruction, applicable reference/brand or the inspected contrast and subject matter supports it. Preserve evidentiary colors and distinctions that cannot rely on color alone.

Adapt direction to the material rather than assigning a universal social-video treatment:

| Editorial need | Useful direction | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education or complex explanation | Progressive diagrams and mechanisms with settled readable labels | Reveal relationships without oversimplifying claims |
| Interview or documentary | Contextual visual development, quiet highlights, unobtrusive identifiers and space for reactions | Do not imply an event or reaction occurred in another context |
| Product demonstration | Actual interface or product detail, tracked callouts | Preserve legibility and show the real interaction |
| Silent screen recording | Selective labels and action-linked callouts | Protect interface text/cursor; use inspected action timing rather than invented speech |
| Travel, action or music-led footage | Source-native shots, movement and sound relationships | Montage, retiming or reordered shots require editing permission |
| Fiction, performance or already polished footage | Quiet, source-grounded graphic development and suitable visual handoffs | Preserve dramatic timing, quiet and intentional visual choices |
| Promotional or energetic short form | Strong hierarchy, selective rhythmic emphasis | Keep important qualifications readable |
| Science, finance, or health | Clearly sourced graphics and explicit uncertainty | Avoid imagery that implies unsupported certainty or outcomes |

For Reference, develop an original implementation of observed attributes, not a copy of a creator's exact sequence, artwork or unlicensed sound. For Manual, implement supplied choices and resolve only genuinely missing choices under Selection. Preserve explicit preset contracts in every mode.

## Map meaning to visual events

Build a per-source plan with these fields: source phrase/word IDs or inspected shot/action interval, semantic purpose, proposed visual and development states, composition/layout, timing anchor and source-to-output mapping, applicable reference attribute/evidence or Auto reason, asset/provenance requirement, protected regions, transition purpose, palette/contrast evidence, adaptation/permission and review concern. Convert validated anchors into output frames through the synchronization reference. Distinguish a concept's onset from an intentional anticipation or delayed reveal.

For each explanatory graphic, the plan must contain these concrete slots before implementation; keep them in the same cue ledger rather than inventing another timeline:

| Slot | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Semantic unit | One small idea with its source evidence; exact heading/concise clause, keyword or symbol; qualifications kept with their claim; previously introduced context versus hidden future material |
| Reveal and reading | Verified speech/action anchor, first semantic visibility, entrance/settled frames, readable hold, exit frames and destination; neutral structural anticipation labeled separately |
| Space and hierarchy | Candidate contiguous regions over the full lifetime, clearance, measured text/group sizes at readable type, selected primary/compact form and content-sized box; fallback when it cannot fit |
| Smooth handoff | Property endpoints/easing, stable persistent elements, resize/travel envelope, old/new visibility scheduling and unchanged source/audio timing |
| Acceptance evidence | Preview includes this unit's hardest timing/space state; final review checks semantic order, actual fit, readability and consecutive transition frames with playback |

Use [semantic reveals and handoffs](motion-design.md) and [space allocation](layouts-effects.md#allocate-space-before-sizing-panels) to fill these slots. A full paragraph shown immediately, a generic fade applied to every row, or “make it smooth and responsive” is not an implementation-ready cue plan. A later highlight cannot undo an early reveal. Explanatory chunking never changes the stationary full-phrase caption contract.

Add a full-duration motion-coverage record using [Motion design](motion-design.md): identify what develops in every passage, what remains settled for reading, how the next state connects, and the interval/reason for any short reading hold. Include the opening, middle, ending, quiet scenes and scene boundaries. Default to evolving meaningful graphics throughout every composition; long frozen cards with only fade-in/fade-out treatment are incomplete. Explicit user requests for stillness remain valid and are recorded with their scope. Continuous development can be quiet and low-amplitude; decorative pulsing, random effects or source cuts/zoom/retiming do not fill a coverage gap.

Choose an asset only when it explains, demonstrates, locates, compares, or supplies relevant atmosphere. Keep the presenter visible when expression is the strongest evidence; keep screen detail/action visible when that is the evidence. Asset selection does not authorize source replacement. There is no B-roll percentage, minimum event count, or fixed interval between cuts. Remove visuals that merely repeat a noun without improving comprehension.

## Compose and pace

Guide attention with a dominant subject, useful negative space, balanced scale and consistent screen direction. Preserve full source framing in Split/Overlay and source-native Adaptive, and the whole visible subject in Cutout. Reposition/resize graphics instead of zooming or cropping the original. Adaptive may change layout by scene while retaining protected content and continuity; it does not waive explicit selected-style contracts or authorize a crop. Match exposure, white balance and saturation across sources before a stylized grade; avoid changing evidentiary color relationships in charts or demonstrations.

Let graphics follow the spoken thought or inspected action, visual complexity and reading time within the protected source clock. Budget entrance, meaningful development, readable hold and exit separately. Settle labels while other graphic parts reveal relationships, advance a mechanism or highlight the current step. Prototype a dense passage and the hardest relevant effect before extending the system. In Reference mode compare the test's applicable typography, layout, motion, pacing and sound with timecoded sample examples; document adaptations and unassessed attributes. Review whether viewers can listen, read and follow motion simultaneously, then simplify overloaded moments without reverting the whole passage to a static card. A passed short test does not certify the full export.

## Strong openings and useful explanations

Use the opening's actual meaning to establish context with a clear title, question or diagram. Do not fabricate a claim, move later speech forward, remove a pause or create a loop solely for retention. Show a source-grounded payoff when the speaker reaches it. Continue meaningful visual development through the rest of the edit; an animated opening does not excuse static middle or ending passages. For short-form viewing, keep legible type, immediate topic clarity and uncluttered information while timing graphic development to meaning rather than a compulsory effect every few seconds.

Plan explanations as entities, actions and outcomes: introduce the relevant object, reveal its relationship or process when spoken, then show the supported consequence. Keep useful context visible while highlighting the active step. A conceptual illustration is not documentary footage. Adapt generic cards into topic-specific visual explanations rather than filling every scene with the same decorative box. No template, asset generator or paid service is mandatory.
