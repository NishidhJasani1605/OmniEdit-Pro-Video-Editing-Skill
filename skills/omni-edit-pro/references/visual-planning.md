# Visual planning

Read after source inspection and transcript review, before acquiring assets or building compositions. Produce a small creative brief and an actionable visual plan in the editing workspace. Use [transcription-sync.md](transcription-sync.md) for timing mechanics and [broll-assets.md](broll-assets.md) for asset research.

## Understand the existing edit

Watch representative passages with audio, including the opening, a dense explanation, an emotional turn, and the ending. Inspect existing cuts, shot changes, demonstrations, captions, and graphics before proposing additions. Describe what the audience needs to understand, what the speaker already communicates visually, and where supporting imagery would add information. Distinguish deliberate quiet from a technical defect; pauses and reactions may carry meaning.

For talking-head and narrated footage, retain the original footage as the continuous underlying track where applicable. Preserve all dialogue, its order, timing, and lip-sync unless the user explicitly requests an editorial change. Full-frame supporting visuals can temporarily cover that track without changing its playback. Do not create jump cuts, remove pauses, or shorten speech to fit a graphic. Other genres follow the user's creative brief.

## Choose a coherent direction

Record the intended audience, viewing context, aspect ratio, language, tone, and desired takeaway. Set a compact visual vocabulary: type hierarchy, palette, contrast treatment, image style, motion character, and recurring layout. Reuse these decisions across scenes; vary emphasis when the story warrants it.

Adapt direction to the material rather than assigning a universal social-video treatment:

| Editorial need | Useful direction | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education or complex explanation | Progressive diagrams, restrained labels, longer reading holds | Reveal relationships without oversimplifying claims |
| Interview or documentary | Contextual imagery, unobtrusive identifiers, space for reactions | Do not imply an event or reaction occurred in another context |
| Product demonstration | Actual interface or product detail, tracked callouts | Preserve legibility and show the real interaction |
| Promotional or energetic short form | Strong hierarchy, selective rhythmic emphasis | Keep important qualifications readable |
| Science, finance, or health | Clearly sourced graphics and explicit uncertainty | Avoid imagery that implies unsupported certainty or outcomes |

Use the user's references to identify attributes such as contrast, rhythm, density, and framing. Develop an original treatment rather than copying a creator's exact sequence or artwork.

## Map meaning to visual events

Build a plan with these fields: source phrase or word IDs, semantic purpose, proposed visual, layout, timing anchor, asset/provenance requirement, protected screen regions, and review concern. Convert validated anchors into output frames through the synchronization reference. Distinguish a concept's spoken onset from an intentional anticipation or delayed reveal.

Choose an asset only when it explains, demonstrates, locates, compares, or supplies relevant atmosphere. Keep the presenter visible when expression is the strongest evidence. There is no B-roll percentage, minimum event count, or fixed interval between cuts. Remove visuals that merely repeat a noun without improving comprehension.

## Compose and pace

Guide attention with a dominant subject, useful negative space, balanced scale, and consistent screen direction. Preserve eye lines, headroom, gestures, and relevant objects when reframing. Match exposure, white balance, and saturation across sources before adding a stylized grade; avoid changing evidentiary color relationships in charts or demonstrations.

Let scene duration follow the spoken thought, visual complexity, and reading time. Budget entrance, readable hold, and exit separately. Prototype the densest passage before extending the system across the video. Review whether the audience can listen, read, and follow motion simultaneously, then simplify any overloaded moment.
